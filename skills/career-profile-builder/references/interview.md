# Choosing the next question

Begin with a breadth inventory: employment, education, projects, open source, volunteering, credentials, languages, community work and optional interests/caregiving. Some categories may not apply. Record concurrent work and partial dates without forcing a linear timeline. Contexts can be named before every detail is known.

Choose depth by usefulness and the person's interest. Prioritize an ownership ambiguity, conflicting source, unclear outcome or important decision over collecting another generic skill. Role preferences, motivations, energy and working conditions belong in profile claims, separately from historical achievements. A target role can remain undecided.

## Episode prompts

Use only the next useful prompt, adapting it to what is already known:

- Concrete memory: “What was happening on one occasion you remember clearly?”
- Goal: “What needed attention, and why did it matter?”
- Ownership: “Which parts did you personally handle?” Later ask which parts others handled.
- Judgment: “Which decision best shows your thinking?” Explore alternatives, constraints and tradeoffs if useful.
- Result: “What happened after your work was used, including anything unchanged or unfinished?”
- Support: “What did you observe that tells you what changed?” Sources can include feedback, adoption, an artifact or a recollection, each labeled accurately.
- Measurement: “Where does that figure come from?” Ask separately for the next missing basis, unit, period, baseline or uncertainty. Scale is not impact. A team result does not prove individual causation.
- Portfolio depth: ask about the audience, iterations, rejected alternatives, limitations and evidence that may be shared. Link artifacts to the particular claims they support.
- Reflection: “What would you change next time?”

For underclaiming, explore routine maintenance, repairs, requests for help, dependencies and informal work. For overclaiming, narrow the statement to the personally supported contribution. Use open factual questions; suggested impressive answers can steer recollection. Offer choices for controls and categories, not invented autobiographical facts.

## Readback and stopping

An episode is ready for readback when there is a recognizable context, a personal action or explicit unknown, an outcome or explicit unknown, and an account of support/uncertainty. None requires a number, prestigious employer or successful outcome. A small episode can be complete while a large achievement remains draft.

Read back the account faithfully before making it confirmed. Keep unresolved differences in a claim's `conflict` note and retain old assertions in history when correcting. Separate exact supplied titles from interpretations. For unclear dates, retain the person's wording; normalize only to supported precision.

After two unsuccessful probes on a fact, offer unknown, defer, another episode or pause. Preserve those as distinct states. A withheld fact needs no explanation; avoid storing a sensitive reason the person chose not to provide. Ask about privacy before treating an artifact, metric, name or wording as publishable.

Finish a session by saying what was saved and what remains open. Do not invent a completeness percentage. On return, inspect the persisted cursor and relevant records rather than replaying the questionnaire. A factual correction may reopen a local group of claims; unrelated contexts retain their confirmation.
